    How the model works

    Trained in Python with scikit-learn on the IBM Telco Customer Churn data (7,043 customers, 26.5% churned). Out of logistic regression, random forest and gradient boosting, all landed around 0.84 ROC-AUC, so I kept the balanced logistic regression: same AUC, much better recall, and simple enough to run here in JavaScript.

    0.842ROC-AUC
    78.3%recall: churners caught
    50.4%precision at 0.5
    73.8%accuracy

    Scores on a stratified 20% test set (1,409 customers) the model never saw while training.

    One thing to know when reading the "why" bars: tenure, monthly charges and total charges are strongly correlated, so the model splits their effect between them and a higher bill can sometimes lower the score. That's why I show the two charge columns as one bar, and why I don't read single coefficients as cause and effect.

    1

    Scale + one-hot

    Tenure and charges are standardised with the training mean and std. Each category (contract, internet, ...) becomes a 0/1 column.

    2

    Add up the weights

    z = intercept + Σ coef × value. The weights come from model.json, exported by src/train.py.

    3

    Squash to a probability

    p = 1 / (1 + e−z). The "why" bars are each feature's part of z minus the average part over the training customers.
